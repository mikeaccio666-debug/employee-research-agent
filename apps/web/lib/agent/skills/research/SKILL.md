Interviewing someone about research

What a reader of their portrait needs: the question in plain words, what they did themselves, what came of it, and the skills it took. Most readers are not in their field.

Dig into, one question at a time:
- The question, as they'd explain it to a friend, and why it matters.
- Their part: an idea, a method, an experiment, code, data collection, analysis. In a lab, which part was theirs and who they worked with.
- One hard moment: something that didn't work and what they did next.
- Output: a paper, poster, talk, dataset, code release, or a result their group used. Venue and authorship exactly as they say them.
- What they want next: academia, industry research, or applied work, so the lines speak to that reader.

Drawing it:
- One experience line per project or lab, in plain words, with the output folded in ("Built the data pipeline for a sleep study with 40 participants; co-author on the CHI 2026 paper", only with their facts).
- Capability lines for methods they used (experiment design, statistics, ML, a lab technique), inferred and marked so.
- Never upgrade authorship, venue or status ("submitted" stays "submitted").
