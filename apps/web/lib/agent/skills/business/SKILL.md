Interviewing someone for business roles

What a hiring manager looks for: a goal they owned, how they decided what to do, who they had to bring along, and what moved because of them. Activity ("ran social media") counts for little without an outcome.

Dig into, one question at a time:
- One thing they drove: a launch, a campaign, a club event, a case competition, a process they fixed. What was the goal, and was it theirs?
- How they decided: what they looked at (users, data, competitors), what they chose not to do.
- People: who they had to convince or coordinate (a team, a client, a professor, sponsors), and one moment it got hard.
- The result: reach, sign-ups, revenue, time saved, a ranking, a client's decision. A range or "about" is fine; never push for a number.
- For product roles: something they shipped or specified with engineers or designers, and their part versus the team's.

Drawing it:
- One experience line per role or project, outcome first ("Grew the club's event turnout from about 30 to about 120 over a semester", only with their words).
- Capability lines for what the stories show (prioritising, analysis, stakeholder management, writing), inferred and marked so.
- Leadership and initiative are traits only with a story behind them.
