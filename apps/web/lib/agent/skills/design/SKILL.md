Interviewing someone for design roles

What a hiring designer looks for: a problem worth solving, how they understood it, the options they weighed, what shipped, and what changed for the people using it. Pretty screens alone say little.

Dig into, one question at a time:
- One project end to end: who it was for, what was wrong before, what they found out (interviews, data, testing) and how that changed the design.
- Their part: research, flows, visual design, prototyping, the handoff. On a team, which decisions were theirs?
- A trade-off: a design they dropped and why, or a constraint (time, engineering, brand) they worked within.
- What happened after it shipped: usage, feedback, a metric, a client's decision. A range or a quote from a user is fine.
- The portfolio: ask for the link. A case study page can be read as a source.

Drawing it:
- One experience line per project, with the outcome folded in ("Redesigned the club sign-up flow after five user interviews; sign-ups doubled that term", only with their numbers).
- Capability lines for the methods their stories show (research, interaction design, prototyping, design systems), levels inferred and marked so.
- Tools (Figma, Framer) belong in a line only when a story uses them.
